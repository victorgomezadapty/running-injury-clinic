01 / QUESTIONFROM MOVEMENT TO PHENOTYPE

THE RESEARCH QUESTION

Which running movement patterns are associated with current injury status?

The project organizes reported injury labels and treadmill kinematics into a transparent, participant-level research workflow. It starts with source-provided summaries and a 30-person engineering pilot, then asks whether robust, diagnosis-aware comparisons are feasible.

↗

This dataset is cross-sectional. It does not show that a movement pattern caused an injury, predicts a future injury, or diagnoses an individual runner.

A DESIGN CHOICE THAT MATTERS

Speed changes the question.

Before comparing biomechanics, account for how fast each runner moved. In the candidate cohort, mean treadmill speed differed between current-status groups. A small experimental study also found speed-related changes in lower-limb joint kinematics and kinetics. The gap is a reason to adjust and test overlap—not evidence that speed caused the group difference.

WHAT THE LITERATURE SAYS

Why phenotype carefully?

Evidence is mixed, definitions vary and study populations matter. The strongest case for this project is not a universal injury marker—it is a transparent way to ask more specific, reproducible questions.

SYSTEMATIC REVIEW · 82 STUDIES

No single gait measure explains running injury.

Lopes and colleagues found only peak hip adduction associated in their pooled analysis; the result was influenced by a small subset of studies. They also highlighted inconsistent measurement definitions and study methods.

Lopes et al., 2023 ↗
POPULATION CHANGES THE SIGNAL

Findings do not transfer automatically.

A prospective-study review reported different patterns by sex and competition level, with limited evidence for particular associations in female recreational runners.

Vannatta et al., 2020 ↗
PROSPECTIVE COHORT · 52 WEEKS

Diagnosis and timing shape interpretation.

In 224 injury-free recreational runners, some baseline factors were associated with medically diagnosed injuries over follow-up. This design answers a different question from current-status comparisons.

52-week prospective cohort ↗
BIOMECHANICS · SPEED

Compare like with like.

Experimental changes in running speed alter peak kinematics and kinetics at the hip, knee and ankle. Speed therefore belongs in the design and interpretation.

Orendurff et al., 2018 ↗
FEASIBILITY · FIELD MEASURES

Small studies are useful when labeled honestly.

A 2025 sprint study paired IMU and force–velocity measures with surveillance, but only two prospective injuries occurred. The authors frame it as feasibility work, not a predictive result.

Wolski et al., 2025 ↗
SAME DATASET · CROSS-SECTIONAL ML

A classifier on this source is already published.

Xiao et al. (2026) reported a random-forest ROC-AUC of 0.790, with high sensitivity and limited specificity. Our complementary angle audits repeated-record labels and cohort timing before future modeling.

Xiao et al., 2026 ↗
↳

Project implication: report diagnosis definitions, speed support, missingness and event-quality gates before any association estimate. Treat exploratory subgroup differences as hypotheses, not screening advice.

INTERPRETATION GUARDRAILS

What this project can—and cannot—say.

+

Can support

  • An auditable description of candidate cohort composition and data quality.
  • Carefully defined, speed-aware associations with current injury labels after event validation.
  • A reproducible starting point for diagnosis-specific research hypotheses.
−

Cannot establish

  • That a movement pattern caused injury or predicts who will be injured later.
  • A validated screening cut-off, clinical diagnosis or treatment recommendation.
  • Independent external validation by a related dataset without a verified participant crosswalk.
